Resources and scholarship guides

Short, practical guides. Always confirm requirements on the official provider website.

How to find fully funded scholarships

Start with official government portals, then university scholarship pages, then supervisor-funded positions.

  • Check each provider's eligibility on its own website
  • Note nationality, degree level and language rules
  • Record deadlines in one place
Government vs professor-funded scholarships

Government awards are competitive and have fixed cycles. Professor-funded positions depend on a supervisor's grant and are often more flexible on timing.

  • Government: central application, set deadline
  • Professor-funded: direct contact with the supervisor
  • Many applicants pursue both
How to contact an international research supervisor

Write a short, specific email showing you have read their work.

  • Clear subject line
  • Two sentences on your background and one on fit
  • Attach a concise CV
  • Follow up once after about two weeks
How to write a strong academic CV

Lead with education and research, keep it factual and easy to scan.

  • Education with dates and grades
  • Research experience and publications
  • Skills and relevant awards
  • Two pages or fewer at early stages
Preparing a statement of purpose

Explain why this subject, why this programme and why you are ready.

  • Be specific about interests
  • Link past work to future goals
  • Avoid generic praise of the university
Scholarship application document checklist

Requirements differ, so start from the official list.

  • Passport and transcripts as required by the provider
  • Degree certificates and CV
  • Recommendation letters
  • Language test results where required
Finding funded PhD research positions

Search university vacancy pages and research job boards, and contact supervisors directly.

  • Match your thesis topic to lab output
  • Check whether the position includes a salary or stipend
  • Confirm language and start-date requirements
Scholarship application mistakes to avoid

Most rejections come from avoidable problems.

  • Missing the deadline or documents
  • Ignoring eligibility rules
  • Sending identical emails to many professors
  • Weak or unclear statements
How to prepare for a scholarship interview

Know your application and your research story.

  • Rehearse a two-minute introduction
  • Be ready to discuss your thesis and future plans
  • Prepare questions for the panel
Planning your study abroad application timeline

Begin early; many cycles open 8 to 12 months before the start of study.

  • Research and shortlist first
  • Prepare tests and documents next
  • Apply, then follow up
  • Always confirm dates on the official site

Official starting points: EducationUSA, DAAD, Study in Korea, Study in Japan.
